# Critical Care

*Also called: ICU, Intensive care*

#### What is critical care?

Critical care is medical care for people who have life-threatening injuries and illnesses. It usually takes place in an intensive care unit (ICU). A team of specially-trained health care providers gives you 24-hour care. This includes using machines to constantly monitor your [vital signs](Vital%20Signs.md). It also usually involves giving you specialized treatments.

#### Who needs critical care?

You need critical care if you have a life-threatening illness or injury, such as:

- Severe [burns](Burns.md)
- [COVID-19](COVID-19%20%28Coronavirus%20Disease%202019%29.md)
- [Heart attack](Heart%20Attack.md)
- [Heart failure](Heart%20Failure.md)
- [Kidney failure](Kidney%20Failure.md)
- People recovering from certain major surgeries
- [Respiratory failure](Respiratory%20Failure.md)
- [Sepsis](Sepsis.md)
- Severe [bleeding](Bleeding.md)
- Serious [infections](Infectious%20Diseases.md)
- Serious [injuries](Wounds%20and%20Injuries.md), such as from car crashes, [falls](Falls.md), and shootings
- [Shock](Shock.md)
- [Stroke](Stroke.md)

#### What happens in a critical care unit?

In a critical care unit, health care providers use lots of different equipment, including:

- Catheters, flexible tubes used to get fluids into the body or to drain fluids from the body
- [Dialysis](Dialysis.md) machines ("artificial kidneys") for people with kidney failure
- Feeding tubes, which give you [nutritional support](Nutritional%20Support.md)
- Intravenous (IV) tubes to give you fluids and medicines
- Machines which check your vital signs and display them on monitors
- [Oxygen therapy](Oxygen%20Therapy.md) to give you extra oxygen to breathe in
- Tracheostomy tubes, which are breathing tubes. The tube is placed in a surgically made hole that goes through the front of the neck and into the windpipe.
- Ventilators (breathing machines), which move air in and out of your lungs. This is for people who have respiratory failure.

These machines can help keep you alive, but many of them can also raise your risk of infection.

Sometimes people in a critical care unit are not able to communicate. It's important that you have an [advance directive](Advance%20Directives.md) in place. This can help your health care providers and family members make important decisions, including [end-of-life decisions](End%20of%20Life%20Issues.md), if you are not able to make them.
