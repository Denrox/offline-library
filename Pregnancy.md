# Pregnancy

You're going to have a baby! It's an exciting time, but it can also feel a bit overwhelming. You may have a lot of questions, including what you can do to give your baby a healthy start. Taking care of your health while you're pregnant will help your fetus develop into a healthy baby. To do that, it's important to:

- **Have regular visits with your health care provider.** During [prenatal care](https://medlineplus.gov/prenatalcare.html) visits your provider checks your health and carefully monitors your pregnancy until you deliver a healthy baby. And if there are any health problems, your provider can find them early. Getting treatment right away can cure many problems and prevent others.
- **Eat healthy and drink plenty of water.** Good [nutrition during pregnancy](https://medlineplus.gov/pregnancyandnutrition.html) includes eating a variety of:

 - Fruits
 - Vegetables
 - Whole grains
 - Lean meats or other protein sources
 - Low-fat dairy products
- **Take prenatal vitamins.** During pregnancy, you need higher amounts of certain vitamins and minerals, such as [folic acid](https://medlineplus.gov/folicacid.html) and [iron](https://medlineplus.gov/iron.html).
- **Be careful with medicines.** Always check with your provider before you start or stop any [medicine](https://medlineplus.gov/pregnancyandmedicines.html). This includes over-the-counter medicines and dietary or herbal supplements.
- **Stay active.** Physical activity can help you stay strong, feel and sleep better, and prepare your body for birth. Check with your provider about which types of activities are right for you.
- **Avoid [substances that could hurt your baby](https://medlineplus.gov/reproductivehazards.html)**, including [alcohol, drugs, and tobacco](https://medlineplus.gov/pregnancyandsubstanceuse.html).

Your body will keep changing as your [fetus develops](https://medlineplus.gov/fetalhealthanddevelopment.html) until your baby is ready to be born. It can be hard to know whether a new symptom is normal or could be a sign of a [problem](https://medlineplus.gov/healthproblemsinpregnancy.html). Check with your provider if something is bothering or worrying you.
