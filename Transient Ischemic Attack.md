# Transient Ischemic Attack

*Also called: Mini-stroke, TIA*

A transient ischemic attack (TIA) is a [stroke](Stroke.md) that lasts only a few minutes. It happens when the blood supply to part of the brain is briefly blocked. Symptoms of a TIA are like other stroke symptoms, but do not last as long. They happen suddenly, and include:

- Numbness or weakness, especially on one side of the body
- Confusion or trouble speaking or understanding speech
- Trouble seeing in one or both eyes
- Difficulty walking
- Dizziness
- Loss of balance or coordination

Most symptoms of a TIA disappear within an hour, although they may last for up to 24 hours. Because you cannot tell if these symptoms are from a TIA or a stroke, you should go to the hospital right away.

TIAs are often a warning sign for future [strokes](Ischemic%20Stroke.md). Taking medicine, such as [blood thinners](Blood%20Thinners.md), may reduce your risk of a stroke. Your doctor might also recommend surgery. You can also help lower your risk by having a [healthy lifestyle](Healthy%20Living.md). This includes not smoking, not drinking too much, eating a healthy diet, and exercising. It is also important to control other health problems, such as [high blood pressure](How%20to%20Prevent%20High%20Blood%20Pressure.md) and [cholesterol](How%20to%20Lower%20Cholesterol.md).
