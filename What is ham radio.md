# What is ham radio?

*Tags: outreach · score 5*

## Question

What is ham radio? (*or, more commonly,* What are those antennas on your car for?)

*I know what ham radio is, of course, but I have a hard time explaining it to people who are not familiar with it. Assume the asker has a typical consumer-level familiarity with regular AM and FM broadcast radio, but does not know what shortwave radio is.*

## Answer (score 6, by webmarc)

Ham radio is a hobby. It's funny because people think that the point of the hobby is specifically to talk to other people with a radio (or even confuse it with broadcast radio or pirate radio).

Other people will tell you that the purpose of the hobby is to "further the radio art" as laid out in the Communications Act of 1934.

But really, as with any hobby, the true purpose is similar to collecting stamps, model ship building, or recreational soccer leagues: to spend time on an activity that has one or more facets the pursuit of which is enjoyable.

In ham radio, some of the facets people pursue include:

- antenna design
- electronic circuitry
- chatting up other hams
- pursing public service using radio
- radio driven remote control
- microwave experimentation
- meeting other hams because it's fun
- figuring how to optimize radio communication with computer aid
- so many more things...

**So my human friendly description for my uninitiated friends is**: it's a chance for me to learn about the technology of radio and meet other peeps who are also into that kind of thing.

Sometimes people will be satisfied with that and we move on. Other times, they'll ask me to unpack it more and I'll go in whatever direction seems to be most fruitful based on what I know about who I'm speaking with.

## Answer (score 2, by Marcus Müller)

What is ham radio? (or, more commonly, What are those antennas on your car for?)

Those are two **extremely** different questions.

What is ham radio?

You wouldn't be asked the first question unless the asker already has context cluing them into thinking you're willing to tell them about ham radio. So, you're either wearing a T-Shirt saying "Ask me about ham radio", or are at a booth at some fair or similar with a banner "ham radio", or you're acquaintances with the asker, and they already have some context for you being involved in ham radio.

In that sense, this seems to be a bit of a hypothetical and underdefined question: an acquaintance would be likely to tell you what they know about you and the mysterious ham radio; on a fair, you'd be in that context (is it a STEM hobby fair? A radio technology trade fair? A university club day?), and you'd start from that context. "What is XYZ? I know nothing!" is a question very rarely exchanged between adult humans.

You would always structure your answer based on the context you share with the asker. There's no single good answer. A communications engineer (and you can definitely get and deserve a good degree in that without having heard of ham radio) will be bored by a lot of the explanations about radio and in fact might get the desire to correct you, but might be interested in experimentation or the social aspects of technically inclined hams. A middle school teacher English teacher might be helplessly overtasked with understanding information on antennas and radio waves, but be very interested in the social and linguistic aspects.

What are those antennas on your car for?

Now, that feels like a very likely question to actually happen.

But then we're already contradicting your next statement:

Assume the asker has a typical consumer-level familiarity with regular AM and FM broadcast radio,

Registering an antenna coming out of your car to be something worth asking you about *severely* outpaces consumer-level familiarity with regular AM and FM broadcast in most industrialized nations.

Many to most modern cars don't even have a whiptail antenna anymore, they have small fins (if at all), I can't remember the last time I listened to AM radio, and while the cars I rented did have have a setting for AM, by no means would anyone in continental Europe ever use that – UHF FM covers all basically places where you'd receive SW AM well enough for enjoyment, and frankly, if you're under 30, you're not using your car radio for much but playing music from your phone, or from the spotify app running on your car's infotainment system itself, if you're driving an upper-class car.

So, if someone approaches you about your antenna, they must have noticed that not only does your car, like about half of modern cars, have an antenna, but also that it's unusually shaped, and that, given the variety of "non-ham" car antennas, yours stands out. That puts them very much in the technically interested bracket of populace.

So, again, great starting point for an explanation! "Oh, good catch! You must have noticed that this antenna is a bit longer than other car antennas; in fact, it's nearly 2m long, a bit shorter actually, because I use it to work the 2m band: you see, I'm a ham radio person…"

To emphasize how important context is:

@Warren's excellent answer states:

It’s a very nerdy, very technical hobby but there are some people who love it and who are not that technical.

Made me chuckle! The selection of hams here is, within the loose community that ham radio forms, comparatively very young, and comparatively technical, but in comparison to, say, people who studied electrical engineering, physics, computer science or math, and that's the context in which *I* usually operate, they are less-than-technical. So, when someone here says "hams are technical", that's partially because they know a lot of technical hams. I also know some hams that simply buy a well-advertised radio, and read publications that are really just high-gloss adverts reviewing technology that they never properly explain to the reader. There's a range of people, and that's fine!

And frankly, nerdy, yes, but in a kind of limited way. I don't *disagree* with Warren's statement, at all, but it really needs to be put in context!

## Answer (score 2, by Warren  VA7WPX)

Ham Radio is a nick-name for a hobby, and for the gear used for that hobby, which is focused mostly on using the parts of the radio frequency spectrum reserved for Amateur Radio Operators, who are known colloquially as “Hams”.

My ham radio call sign is VA7 WPX, and that call sign identifies me, like an on-air badge, to other Hams.

The most important part of the hobby to most Ham Radio operators is owning and using radios, antennas, and associated gear, of which there is a lot. It’s a very nerdy, very technical hobby but there are some people who love it and who are not that technical. It’s the hobby of 1000 sub-hobbies. Some of us love to home-build gear, and antennas, even build our own radios. Some of us like to climb mountains and light the peak of the mountain up with Radio Frequency energy. Some of us like to use radios from boats. Some like to do radio stuff at national and state parks. Some of us like to help in auxiliary communications (be marshalls at parades). Some like to help during times of local emergencies (hurricanes, floods). Some of us like to get involved in research, and science, learning more about radio propagation, or the layers of our upper atmosphere (the ionosphere) or about various topics in electronics, engineering, physics, and so on. Some of us are interested in radio-astronomy, and some are interested in making trips to weird little islands and rocks in the middle of the ocean so we can help people make radio contacts to entities/regions they haven’t contacted before.
