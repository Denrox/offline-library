# Bleeding

*Also called: Hematoma, Hemorrhage*

Bleeding is the loss of [blood](Blood.md). It can be external, or outside the body, like when you get a cut or [wound](Wounds%20and%20Injuries.md). It can also be internal, or inside the body, like when you have an injury to an internal organ. Some bleeding, such as [gastrointestinal bleeding](Gastrointestinal%20Bleeding.md), coughing up blood, or [vaginal bleeding](Vaginal%20Bleeding.md), can be a symptom of a disease.

Normally, when you are injured and start bleeding, a [blood clot](Blood%20Clots.md) forms to stop the bleeding quickly. Afterwards, the clot dissolves naturally. To be able to make a clot, your blood needs blood proteins called clotting factors and a type of blood cell called [platelets](Platelet%20Disorders.md). Some people have a problem with clotting, due to another medical condition or an inherited disease. There are two types of problems:

- Your blood may not form clots normally, known as a [bleeding disorder](Bleeding%20Disorders.md). This happens when your body does not make enough platelets or clotting factors, or they don't work the way they should.
- Your blood may make too many clots, or the clots may not dissolve properly

Sometimes bleeding can cause other problems. A [bruise](Bruises.md) is bleeding under the skin. Some [strokes](Hemorrhagic%20Stroke.md) are caused by bleeding in the brain. Severe bleeding may require [first aid](First%20Aid.md) or a trip to the [emergency room](Emergency%20Medical%20Services.md).
