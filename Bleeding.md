# Bleeding

*Also called: Hematoma, Hemorrhage*

Bleeding is the loss of [blood](https://medlineplus.gov/blood.html). It can be external, or outside the body, like when you get a cut or [wound](https://medlineplus.gov/woundsandinjuries.html). It can also be internal, or inside the body, like when you have an injury to an internal organ. Some bleeding, such as [gastrointestinal bleeding](https://medlineplus.gov/gastrointestinalbleeding.html), coughing up blood, or [vaginal bleeding](https://medlineplus.gov/vaginalbleeding.html), can be a symptom of a disease.

Normally, when you are injured and start bleeding, a [blood clot](https://medlineplus.gov/bloodclots.html) forms to stop the bleeding quickly. Afterwards, the clot dissolves naturally. To be able to make a clot, your blood needs blood proteins called clotting factors and a type of blood cell called [platelets](https://medlineplus.gov/plateletdisorders.html). Some people have a problem with clotting, due to another medical condition or an inherited disease. There are two types of problems:

- Your blood may not form clots normally, known as a [bleeding disorder](https://medlineplus.gov/bleedingdisorders.html). This happens when your body does not make enough platelets or clotting factors, or they don't work the way they should.
- Your blood may make too many clots, or the clots may not dissolve properly

Sometimes bleeding can cause other problems. A [bruise](https://medlineplus.gov/bruises.html) is bleeding under the skin. Some [strokes](https://medlineplus.gov/hemorrhagicstroke.html) are caused by bleeding in the brain. Severe bleeding may require [first aid](https://medlineplus.gov/firstaid.html) or a trip to the [emergency room](https://medlineplus.gov/emergencymedicalservices.html).
