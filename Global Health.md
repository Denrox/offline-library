# Global Health

*Also called: International health*

The spread of a disease doesn't stop at a country's borders. With more people living in crowded cities and [traveling](https://medlineplus.gov/travelershealth.html) to other countries, it's easier for germs to spread. [Infectious diseases](https://medlineplus.gov/infectiousdiseases.html) that start in one part of the world can quickly reach another part of the world. Some of the major diseases currently affecting countries around the globe include [HIV](https://medlineplus.gov/hiv.html), [malaria](https://medlineplus.gov/malaria.html), [COVID-19](https://medlineplus.gov/covid19coronavirusdisease2019.html), and [tuberculosis](https://medlineplus.gov/tuberculosis.html).

Besides the spread of diseases, there are other global health challenges. Resistance to medicines such as [antibiotics](https://medlineplus.gov/antibioticresistance.html) is on the rise. That makes it harder to treat certain diseases. Natural and man-made disasters create refugee populations with immediate and long-term health problems. [Climate change](https://medlineplus.gov/climatechange.html) and not enough access to food and safe water are also global problems which can affect people's health. Many countries and health organizations are working together and sharing information on these and other health issues.
