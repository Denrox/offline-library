# Effects of various building materials on a balanced transmission line?

*Tags: transmission-line · score 3*

## Question

Is there a significant change in impedance, and/or loss, when balanced transmission line is placed through non-metallic building materials?

e.g., plaster, gypsum wallboard, wood, or cementitious siding?

## Answer (score 3, by hobbs - KC2G)

Yes, if the distance between the transmission line and the other object is less than 1 or 2 times the the distance between its conductors, there's likely to be some effect. The effect will be greater the longer the length of transmission line that's near the other object (i.e. if it's just a few inches, it's rarely anything worth thinking about for either loss or impedance purposes, at HF frequencies).

I don't know of any decent models for this stuff, but you can get an idea by searching for various materials together with "permittivity" or "dielectric constant" (which will give you some idea of its effect on impedance) and "loss tangent" (which will similarly inform you about lossiness). Anything with a high moisture content is likely to be lossy, which includes wood most of the time, and probably some plasters, gypsums, and cements some of the time.
