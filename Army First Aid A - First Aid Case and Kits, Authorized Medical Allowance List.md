### Army Combat Lifesaver Bag

A-7. The U.S. Army CLS carries a small aid bag, also classified as a medical equipment set (MES). It contains supplies for controlling bleeding, relieving tension pneumothorax, and performing other procedures. The CLS bag authorization is contained in CTA 8-100, with unit basis of issue plan of one per: squad, crew, team as determined by the unit commander. For a current listing of the supplies found in the MES refer to the Army Medical Logistics Command website on the Medical Materiel Information Portal tab, which is managed by United States Army Medical Materiel Agency. The prescribed packing list can be located under unit assemblage code (UAC) 245# nomenclature of MES CLS. The # in the UAC code is a place holder as the last character is changed as the assemblage list is updated. At the time of publication, the current version of the MES CLS is UAC 245C. Table A-4 on page 220 is the listing of supplies for the MES CLS with UAC

245C. The most recent and active assemblage list should be used when inventorying and packing the MES CLS.

### United States Marine Corps Individual First Aid Kit

A-9. The USMC IFAK provides individual Marines and Soldiers with the ability to perform self-aid for lifethreatening arterial bleeding and common non-life-threatening injuries. It also provides the capability to treat non-potable water for personal consumption.

A-10. The IFAK is made up of a trauma kit for airway and bleeding injuries and an accessory kit containing supplies for non-life-threatening injuries and potable water disinfection.

A-11. Component inventories are provided with each IFAK. Control, reliability, readiness, issue, and recovery of the IFAK for the Marine expeditionary forces are the responsibility of the supporting individual issue facility. Control, reliability, readiness, issues, and recovery of the IFAK for units not supported by individual issue facility are the responsibility of the using unit.
